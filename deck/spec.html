01Where the project came from

There was no pre-set project. The shape was designed live in a meeting on 13 to 14 July.

The programme listing read “Reasoning enhancement in large language models for healthcare applications”, deliberately broad. Three things then narrowed it, each traceable to a specific conversation.

1. The supervisor's standing interest, 13 June

Recently, I found sycophancy … and I wonder if we could detect and correct it in healthcare settings.Prof. Tingting Zhu, by email, with two papers attached

2. The collaborator's structure, 23 July

You could use one LLM to display supporter, and another one is opponent. And after discussion to see if they could reach agreement … we don't need doctors to participate. Although the final results maybe wrong, the thinking and discussion process are meaningful.Zhikang Chen

3. The objection that produced the whole design

The doctor makes the right decision, that's a huge assumption you make.Prof. Zhu, on the first version of the design

That objection is the reason the study is built around a laboratory susceptibility panel rather than around agreement with the treating clinician. It is the single most consequential piece of feedback in the project.

02The research questions

Primary question, the supervisor's words, verbatim

Does multi-agent communication improve clinical decision quality, or does it merely make the models agree?

Four sub-questions follow from it, and each maps to an experimental arm:

#Sub-questionArm that answers it
Q1Does a single agent's recommendation depend on the patient at all?Zero-shot round-0, plus encoder baselines and self-consistency
Q2When challenged by a peer, does an agent hold a verifiably correct position?Two-agent debate, both speaking orders
Q3Does it revise correctly when given genuinely new evidence?Panel-reveal arm, and clean-context reveal
Q4Is any movement caused by pressure, by argument content, or by the instruction?Neutral control, content-degraded control, prompt-clause ablation

The headline is the gap between Q2 and Q3, between capitulating to assertion and revising under evidence. Those are different failures and the design separates them.

03Design decisions, and why each was made

DecisionWhat was chosenWhy
PopulationAdult bloodstream infectionFirst choice was urinary infection, rejected: “UTI is too easy. It doesn't need to be tested with culture.” Uncomplicated UTI follows a fixed guideline, so the culture rarely changes management and there is no decision gap to study.
Ground truthLaboratory susceptibility panel, organism × antibiotic × S/I/RGives a correct answer independent of what the clinician did. Answers the “huge assumption” objection directly.
Decision pointThe moment cultures are sent, before any resultGrades the model on the same information the clinician had. The culture returning becomes the new-evidence test.
Two personasInfectious disease specialist vs antimicrobial stewardship leadZhikang's design: identities with “a clear, potentially conflicting incentive”, one wants coverage, the other restraint, to make deference visible.
Both speaking ordersEvery case run A-first and B-firstTurns speaking order into a measurable variable rather than an uncontrolled one. Pairs every comparison within patient.
Answer spaceClosed 17-drug formulary, plus OTHER and ABSTAINAn off-list answer becomes a parse failure, never a silently wrong answer.
ModelQwen3-4B-instruct, 4-bit, localZhikang: “start from the Qwen family, because you could use them freely.” Local execution is also required by the data use agreement.
DeterminismTemperature 0, fixed seed 20260818A repeated run reproduces the same text, so any difference between arms is the arm.
Pre-registrationCohort content-hashed and scorer SHA-pinned before the first model callNothing can be tuned after seeing a result. This is what makes every downstream number defensible.

Round-0 carries no debate framing anywhere, which is what keeps the zero-shot position uncontaminated by the knowledge that a challenge is coming. The scaffold is hash-pinned and asserted on every launch, so a silent edit aborts the run rather than producing results under a changed instrument.

The result that proves her hierarchy was right

Mortality was computed, and split by whether the agent's final recommendation was adequate: 19.5% 30-day mortality where the agent was right, 15.2% where it was wrong. Patients the agent got right died more often, because sicker patients grow more organisms and get fuller panels. That is severity confounding in one line, and it is empirical support for demoting mortality rather than an argument from principle.

06The collaborator's specification

Zhikang Chen's brief, 23 July. All nine elements satisfied.

Start by operationalising sycophancy with clear, measurable indicators, for example, how often a model changes its initial stance after the dialogue, how uncritically it accepts the other agent's arguments, and how far its final recommendation deviates from evidence-based guidelines.Zhikang Chen

Indicator 3 uses the WHO AWaRe classification (Access / Watch / Reserve) , published, externally maintained, and not model-generated, which satisfies the project rule against improvised guideline flags. He also asked for per-turn stance tracking, which produced the finding below.

Per-turn stance, and it is the persona, not the turn order

Agent A abandons its own position in 400/400 runs, whether it opens or responds. Agent B abandons in 200/400, it folds when it opens and never once when it responds. Identical structural position, opposite behaviour. The stewardship lead holds its ground; the infectious disease specialist never does. The two identities were assigned precisely to create conflicting incentives, and they behave differently.

Model against clinician, on comparable ground

Her question, 30 July: “You can also compare LLM with clinician, see if they agree or LLM is worse or better?”

all 200 casescases where both can be scored
Clinician's actual empiric prescription125/200 = 62.5%125/138 = 90.6%
Model, round-0175/200 = 87.5%175/192 = 91.1%

The 25-point margin on the full cohort is a denominator artefact. The clinician scores UNDETERMINED in 62 of 200 cases, largely because real prescriptions fall outside the closed 17-drug formulary or were never tested against the isolate, so that comparison penalises the clinician for prescribing outside the model's answer space. On the cases where both can be scored the two are indistinguishable: 91.1% against 90.6%. The answer to her question is that neither is better.

Context that belongs beside it: susceptibility results become available at a median of 134 hours after the culture is drawn, and zero are available at 5, 12 or 24 hours. The empiric decision is genuinely made without the information, by the clinician and the model alike. That is why the decision point sits before the culture returns, a design choice that came from the supervisor directly: “it is interesting to see what LLM suggests before the culture result is out.”

Stewardship: what the AWaRe result does and does not say

All 400 recommendations, before and after the conversation, sit in the WHO Watch group. That is not presented as a failure, Watch-group empiric therapy is the clinically expected choice for suspected bacteraemia, and Access-group agents would be inappropriate for this indication. The finding is the absence of movement:

The defensible claim

The debate changes the recommended drug in 400/400 runs and changes the WHO AWaRe class in 0/400. Five turns of conversation reshuffle within a single stewardship tier. Whatever the exchange is doing, it is not de-escalation.

A policy that never looks at the patient scores 96%. That is the measurement-validity finding: coverage reported without a degenerate-policy comparator carries no information about whether the model conditioned on the patient.

McNemar exact p = 3.0 × 10⁻⁸, discordance entirely one-directional. Removing the deference-inviting clause from the prompt leaves adoption at 60/60, the behaviour is dispositional, not instructed.

09What I could not do in July

The honest arc. Each line is something that had to be learned before the next became possible.

WEEK 1
Could not access the data at all. Asked the collaborator whether applying for MIMIC was “a button on this page”. Did not know PhysioNet, CITI training, or the difference between MIMIC-III and IV.
WEEK 2
Chose a population because it was convenient. Picked urinary infections for volume and clean labs. Rejected in one line, and it took a day to understand that an easy dataset is not the same as a question worth asking.
WEEK 3
Learned what a ground truth has to be. That checking a model against the clinician only measures agreement, not correctness, and that the susceptibility panel gives an answer independent of both.
WEEK 4
Learned to freeze before you look. Content-hashed the cohort and pinned the scorer before running a single model call, so nothing could be tuned after seeing a result.
WEEK 5
Learned that a clean number is a warning. Found a perfect 160/160 in my own results, traced it to a safety check that was deleting 220 runs non-randomly, fixed it, and watched the real figure appear: every one of the 311 runs that reached the panel on an adequate drug stayed on one.
WEEK 5
Learned why controls come first. Built the neutral re-ask arm and got 0/200. Without that zero, “it folds every time” could have meant “it is unstable”.
WEEK 6
Learned to attack my own best result. A seeded arm gave an impressive discrimination score. A drug-matched control showed the same drug was refused whether the panel called it susceptible or resistant, the effect was zero, and the number came from which drugs happened to be resistant.
WEEK 6
Learned that denominators are where the errors live. Nearly every defect found in this project was a denominator: a rate over intended n rather than actual, a comparison between one column reused and another freshly computed, a subset presented as a cohort.

10What is missing

GapStatusWhy it matters
Single agent at matched computedoneFive independent samples per case, majority vote, turn-for-turn matched, complete at 200 cases. It was the largest hole: without it, “two agents perform like one” is partly entailed by the 100% abandonment rather than independently measured.
Few-shot progressiondoneBoth rungs complete on the same 200 cases. Few-shot breaks the constant, 1 drug to 6, and significantly degrades coverage, exact McNemar p = 0.0004. That is the result that licenses fine-tuning.
Label-space breadthlimitationOnly 3 of 17 formulary drugs ever appear as a final answer, so the 41% order effect is a flip on an effectively two-way choice. Must be stated.
Cohort selectionlimitation200/200 cases are microbiology-evaluable because they were selected on having an interpretable panel. Published comparators report 32%. This is a declared post-baseline selection.
Clustering in intervalslimitationConfidence intervals treat ordering-runs as independent when they are patients seen twice, so every interval is narrower than the design supports.
Model scopelimitationOne 4B model as the subject, one as a robustness check. Findings are scoped to these checkpoints, not to language models generally.
Hosted-model transferblockedThe collaborator suggested transferring the protocol to GPT once it works. The data use agreement forbids sending record-level data to a hosted service, so this needs synthetic cases.
