## 5. The framing risk

**The strongest case that "sycophancy" is the wrong word.** Every quantity offered as evidence of deference is entailed by the instrument rather than measured against it. Agent B's system prompt says "Counter the recommendation if you disagree, or concur if you agree"; Agent A's says "Consider their comments and state your position". One agent is instructed to counter, the other to consider, and that is exactly what they do: B-first t3 differs from t1 in 200/200, and every other turn in both orderings adopts the most recent counterpart drug. Before any interaction the two personas already agree on 199 of 200 cases, both opening on pip-tazo, so there is no persona difference to defer across. The label space has three members in practice, so "adopting the counterpart's drug" is a coin with two sides. Of the 800 A-response turns, 200 are turns where the counterpart's drug already equals A's own previous position (adopting and holding are the same act) and a further 199 are turns where the counterpart's drug is A's own constant default, only 401 are informative. The clause ablation that would separate instruction from disposition is at ceiling, 60/60 in both arms, and no persona-swap arm exists anywhere in the repository. On this evidence the phenomenon is instruction-following inside a degenerate answer space, and "sycophancy" imports a disposition the design cannot see.

**The best response, without softening it.** The harm endpoint does not depend on the label. In 52 of 350 runs the agent moves from a drug the laboratory called S to a drug the laboratory called R, and the destination tested R in **52 of 52**, with pip-tazo S in 52 of 52. Coverage falls from 350/400 to 312/400, a 9.5-point drop with McNemar p = 2.5e-3, and it falls in the direction of an external arbiter that no part of the pipeline can influence. Speaking order alone changes the final drug in 82 of 200 cases with 81 of the 82 flips running the same way. A system whose recommendation is decided by turn position, and which moves onto a resistant agent because a counterpart asserted it, is unsafe to deploy whatever the underlying mechanism is called. The defensible framing is the narrow one: **position abandonment under counterpart assertion, scored against laboratory susceptibility**. That claim is fully identified by the data on disk. The disposition claim is not, and should be dropped from the deck rather than defended.

## 6. The one question this project cannot answer

**"Is the deference a property of the model, or of the two instruction sentences you wrote?"**

Nothing on disk separates them. The two system prompts differ in four ways beyond the job title, one of which is a deference cue ("Consider their comments") and one a licence to dissent ("Counter the recommendation if you disagree"). The single arm built to test this, `ablation_pass.py`, which removes the consideration clause from Agent A, returns 60/60 adoption in both arms, so it has no power to detect an effect of any size; `LITERATURE_PRESSURE_TEST.md` C7 says so in the project's own words. There is no persona-swap arm. The 199/200 pre-interaction agreement shows the two personas are behaviourally identical until the prompt tells one of them to counter, which points at the instruction rather than the model, but points is all it does.

**The honest response in the room:** "I cannot separate them with what I have. The ablation is at ceiling in both arms, so it has zero power, and there is no swap arm. The experiment that identifies it is 60 cases with the two instruction sentences exchanged and the job titles held fixed, under ten minutes of compute at my measured 9.5 seconds per case. It is the first thing I run after this talk, and until it exists I am claiming position abandonment under counterpart assertion, not a disposition."
